The problem

Satellites only ever see the ocean's skin.

Temperature, height, salinity, and currents at the surface — that's the limit of what orbits above can measure. Underneath, where heat is stored, where cyclones draw their fuel, and where fish actually live, satellites see nothing at all.

The idea

OceanEmbed reconstructs what's missing.

Twelve satellite-observable surface signals — temperature, salinity, height, currents, and wind — go into a custom deep learning model, OceanUNet, trained to map what's on the surface to what's happening below it, down to 902 metres.

The proof

Validated against real ARGO floats, not just held-out data.

Coverage spans the North Indian Ocean — 5°N to 30°N, 45°E to 105°E — at a daily, 0.25° grid.
